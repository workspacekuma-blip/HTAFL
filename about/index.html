The founder’s story

Why HTAFL exists.

The story behind Hope. Talent. Art. Fashion. Life.

I remember where the idea for HTAFL began.

It was in my hostel room, sometime during my final year, with music playing in the background and the peculiar loneliness that comes when you are surrounded by people and yet feel entirely alone.

I have always taken refuge in music. There are things I have never been able to say , not because I did not feel them, but because the words would not come. Then a song would play, and suddenly someone else had said them for me. It was almost frightening, how accurately a stranger's voice could enter a place inside me that I had spent years trying to hide.

And that night, I began to wonder about people who had no song.

People who carry things inside themselves for years and have no language for them. People who wake up every morning with something heavy sitting quietly on their chest, something they cannot explain to their families, their friends, perhaps not even to themselves.

I understood that kind of silence.

I had been fighting depression and anxiety since I was young. And when the weight became unbearable, I would search desperately for somewhere to put it. Somewhere anywhere that was not inside me.

That was how I began writing.

Not because I believed I was good at it. In fact, I had been told quite plainly that I wasn't. But perhaps that was never the point. I wrote because the words belonged to me. They were imperfect, sometimes ugly, sometimes embarrassing, but they were mine. And for a person who has spent so much of his life feeling powerless over what happens inside him, there is something almost sacred about creating one small thing that belongs entirely to you.

Growing up, I envied artists.

I thought they possessed some secret I had been denied. I believed art had rules. It had to look a certain way, be taught a certain way, be beautiful in the correct way. If it was not technically good, then it did not deserve to exist.

I carried that belief for years.

Then, one day, I came across an Instagram page that posted children's drawings with little explanations of what they meant.

There was one drawing of a family.

The mother stood in the centre, and her arms stretched impossibly wide wide enough to reach every one of her children.

Technically, it was not a beautiful drawing.

A trained artist could have looked at it and told you everything that was wrong: the proportions, the anatomy, the lines, the colours, the perspective. They could have corrected it until it became something that looked more like art.

But they would have destroyed the most important thing about it.

Because those enormous arms were exactly how that child saw his mother.

Her arms were not anatomically correct.

They were protection.

They were home.

They were the child's way of saying, My mother can reach all of us. No matter how far we are from her, she can still hold us.

And I remember thinking: perhaps we have misunderstood art.

Perhaps the question was never, Is this beautiful?

Perhaps the question is, Why did someone need to make this?

That is where HTAFL was born.

Not from the belief that everyone can become a great artist, but from something much simpler and, perhaps, much more human:

You do not have to be good at creating something for what you create to mean something.

The beauty is sometimes not in the stroke, the voice, the photograph, the garment, the movement or the finished piece.

Sometimes the beauty is in the fact that someone was carrying something they could not survive carrying aloneand they found a way to put it somewhere outside themselves.

That is what HTAFL  is built on.

Not perfection.

Not talent as the world defines it.

But the why behind the creation.

The wound behind the painting.

The memory behind the photograph.

The grief behind the song.

The anger behind the design.

The silence behind the words.

The story the art is actually trying to tell.

At first, I called it HATE TAKEN AWAY FOR LIFE.

I thought that was what I wanted to speak about the hatred people carry, the darkness that grows inside us when pain has nowhere to go.

But eventually I understood that if I called it that, we might spend all our time staring at the darkness itself.

And that was never the point.

The point was what we do with it.

What happens when you take the thing that is destroying you and, instead of allowing it to destroy someone else, you transform it.

So the name changed.

HOPE. TALENT. ART. FASHION. LIFE.

HTAFL.

Because perhaps hope is not always something we find.

Perhaps sometimes we have to make it.

Perhaps the things that nearly broke us can become the very things through which someone else finally breathes.

And perhaps art, in the end, is not about making something beautiful.

Perhaps it is simply the human refusal to let pain be meaningless.

You take what you have been carrying.

You give it a shape.

You leave it somewhere outside yourself.

And maybe, somewhere, someone who has been carrying the same invisible weight sees it and thinks:

So I am not the only one.

Maybe that is where closure begins.                                 the beginning.

Our mission

Fashion Meets Healing

At HTAFL, we unite children and young adults experiencing hate, oppression, depression, anxiety, and hopelessness through the transformative power of fashion and art. Our mission is to create safe, creative spaces where young people can express their authentic selves, build resilience, and discover healthier approaches to living. Through artistic expression and fashion-forward initiatives, we foster understanding, self-acceptance, and community connection. HTAFL empowers the next generation to transform personal struggles into creative strength, inspiring positive change and breaking cycles of negativity. We are committed to building a world where every young person can thrive free from hate, with confidence in their unique identity and creative potential.

Our vision

Room to become yourself.

A world where every young person can grow free from hate, feel a sense of belonging and build confidence in their identity and creative potential.
